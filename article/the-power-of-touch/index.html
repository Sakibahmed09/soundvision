The Power of Touch

Abu Hudhayfah Edwards

From the archive · 5 min read

SUMMARY: Explore the importance of a compassionate touch with the story of Prophet Ayyub, peace be upon him.

When most people hear the phrase “the power of touch,” they think of a warm hug, a handshake between friends, a parent comforting a frightened child, or a gentle pat on the shoulder during a difficult moment. Islam certainly recognizes the importance of these acts of kindness, but before exploring the Sunnah of compassionate touch, it is worth beginning with one of the greatest examples of patience found in the Qur’an: the story of Prophet Ayyub (Job) عليه السلام.

The story of Ayyub teaches us that touch can mean many things. It can be the touch of hardship. It can be the touch of compassion. It can be the touch of healing. Most importantly, it reminds us of the touch of Allah’s Mercy.

Allah says in Surah Al-Anbiya (Quran 21:83–84):

“And (remember) Ayyub, when he cried to his Lord: ‘Verily, distress has seized me, and You are the Most Merciful of all those who show mercy.’ So We answered his call, and We removed the distress that was on him, and We restored his family to him, and the like thereof along with them, as a mercy from Ourselves and a Reminder for all who worship Us.”

Notice the remarkable dignity in Ayyub’s supplication. He does not complain about Allah’s decree, nor does he ask, “Why me?” He simply describes his condition while praising the mercy of his Lord. Even in the depths of suffering, his heart remained connected to Allah.

Later, Allah relates another part of his story in Surah Sad (Quran 38:41–42):

“And remember Our slave Ayyub, when he invoked his Lord: ‘Verily! Shaitan has touched me with distress and torment!’ (Allah said to him): ‘Strike the ground with your foot. This is water to wash in, cool and a [refreshing] drink.’”

Here we see the first kind of touch: the touch of hardship. Every believer experiences trials. Illness, loss, disappointment, and grief visit every household sooner or later. Yet Ayyub teaches us that patience is not the absence of pain; it is remaining devoted to Allah while enduring it.

The second kind of touch is the touch of compassion. According to the narrative quoted by Imam Imaduddin Ismail Ibn Kathir in his Tafsir of Surah Sad (Quran 38:41–44), which he relates from the Tabai Wahb ibn Munabbih, Ayyub’s wife remained steadfast beside her husband throughout his prolonged illness. The report mentions that because he had become so weak, she would take him by the hand, help him walk, and return him home while continuing to care for him. This report is not found in the Qur’an or in an authentic hadith of the Prophet ﷺ, but is among the historical narrations cited by Ibn Kathir in his commentary. Whether every detail is established or not, the Quran itself bears witness to the remarkable patience and faith of Ayyub and the restoration of his family by Allah’s mercy.

Sometimes the greatest act of compassion is not finding the perfect words. Sometimes it is simply refusing to let go of someone’s hand. Sitting beside a hospital bed, helping an elderly parent walk across a room, embracing a grieving friend, or quietly reminding a loved one that they are not facing hardship alone can all become acts of worship when done seeking Allah’s pleasure.

The third kind of touch is the touch of healing. Allah commanded Ayyub to strike the ground with his foot, and from it came cool water for washing and drinking. The healing came from Allah alone, but He chose to heal His Prophet through means. The cool water touched tired skin, refreshed his body, and by Allah’s permission became part of his recovery. It is a reminder that while we place our trust in Allah, we also take the means He has provided.

The fourth kind of touch is the touch of blessing. Abu Hurayrah (رضي الله عنه) narrated that the Prophet ﷺ said that while Ayyub was bathing, golden locusts began falling upon him. Ayyub gathered them into his garment, whereupon Allah asked, “O Ayyub ! Have I not made you rich enough so that you have no need of what you see?”

Ayyub replied, “Yes, by Your Might! But I cannot do without Your blessing.” (Sahih Bukhari, 3391; Kitab al-Tawhid, 7493)

Finally, Allah instructed Ayyub regarding an oath he had made during his illness:

“And take in your hand a bundle of thin grass and strike therewith (your wife), and break not your oath.” (Quran, 38:44)

Allah showed Ayyūb a merciful way to fulfill his oath without injuring his wife, the woman who had faithfully stood beside him through years of hardship. Even here, the story concludes not with anger, but with mercy.

The story of Ayyub reminds us that the power of touch is not limited to physical contact. Hardship may touch a believer, but so can patience. A loving spouse may take a trembling hand. Cool water may touch tired skin. Allah may remove pain and replace it with mercy. And through every trial, His blessing remains greater than anything we could ever gather with our own hands.

Perhaps that is the greatest lesson of all. The most powerful touch in our lives is not the one we give or receive from another human being. It is the mercy of Allah that touches the heart of His believing servant, strengthening it to endure every trial until relief finally comes.

First Day of School

The little lavender backpack rested beside the front door, packed neatly with crayons, pencils, a notebook, and a lunchbox decorated with butterflies. Five-year-old Asiya had spent the past week putting it on, taking it off, and asking the same question over and over.
Abu Hudhayfah Edwards

From the archive · 5 min read

“Am I really going to school tomorrow?”

“You really are…” her father replied with a smile. “ but your first day is Monday, In sha Allah”

Asiya giggled, hugged her backpack, and hurried off to show it to her teddy bear for what seemed like the hundredth time that week.

The house grew quiet.

Her mother folded one last outfit and placed it carefully into a drawer.

“I wish…” she whispered.

Her husband looked up from the stack of school papers spread across the kitchen table.

“I wish we could have sent her to Islamic school.”

He nodded slowly.

“So do I.”

Months earlier, they had toured a nearby Islamic school. They loved the classrooms, the Quran lessons, the smiling teachers, and hearing children greet one another with As-salamu alaykum in the hallways.

But after adding the tuition, registration fees, uniforms, transportation, and books, they reached the same conclusion every time.

It simply wasn’t possible.

Not this year.

Maybe someday, bi-idhnillaah.

But not today.

Mama sighed as she looked toward Asiya’s bedroom.

“I feel guilty.”

“Don’t.” Abi reached across the table and gently held her hand.

“Allah doesn’t hold us accountable for what He hasn’t provided. He asks us to be faithful with what He has entrusted to us.”

She nodded, though the feeling lingered.

“I know. I just wish our best looked different.”

He smiled gently.

“So do I.”

“But perhaps Allah has good waiting for her there that we cannot yet see.”

Those words settled comfortably between them.

Neither pretended the disappointment wasn’t real. It was. Yet so was their trust that Allah knew what was best for their family.

For now, public school was simply the door that Allah had opened.

That evening they talked about everything parents usually worry about before a child’s first day of school.

Would she make friends?

Would she remember to say Bismillah before eating?

Would she know to be kind even if others were not?

Would she tell her teacher if something bothered her?

Then Mama asked the question that had quietly been sitting in both of their hearts.

“What about lunch?”

Abi looked at the enrollment papers.

“What if they accidentally give her pork?”

Neither of them knew.

The registration packet sat on the table, nearly complete.

Nearly.

The following afternoon, the telephone rang.

“Hello, this is Mrs. Carter, the teacher’s assistant at Oak Grove Elementary. May I speak with Mr. or Mrs. Rashid?”

“This is she.”

“I was reviewing Asiya’s registration packet and noticed one section wasn’t completed.”

Mama’s heart skipped.

“Oh dear…what did we forget?”

“The cafeteria meal selection.”

“The meal selection?”

“Yes, ma’am. Before school starts, every family chooses which meal option they would like for their child.”

Mama reached for a pen.

Mrs. Carter continued kindly.

“We currently offer four choices.”

She read them one by one.

“Regular.”

“Kosher.”

“Halal.”

“Vegetarian.”

For a moment, Mama simply blinked.

Across the room, Abi could tell from the look on her face exactly what she was hearing.

He smiled before she even repeated the words.

“We…we can choose the halal option?” Mama asked.

“Absolutely,” Mrs. Carter replied. “We just need you to mark it on the form. And if your family’s needs ever change, just let us know. We can update it.”

After thanking her several times, Mama hung up the phone and looked at her husband.

“We spent three days worrying…”

“…And the answer was waiting on a form we hadn’t finished.”

Abi laughed softly.

“I guess we weren’t the first parents to ask.”

“I’m certain you weren’t,” Mama replied with a smile.

Then her expression became thoughtful.

“You know…”

“What?”

“I already feel better.”

“So do I.”

She gathered the school papers into a folder.

“But one phone call doesn’t answer every question.”

Abi nodded.

“No. It doesn’t.”

They decided to make a list for Kindergarten Open House. They wanted to introduce themselves to Asiya’s teacher, learn how the school communicated with families, ask about classroom celebrations, and let the staff know they welcomed open communication throughout the year. Just as importantly, they wanted Asiya to know she could always come to them if something at school made her uncomfortable or confused.

“This isn’t just about dropping her off,” Mama said.

“It’s about building a relationship,” Abi replied.

The first morning of school arrived sooner than either of them expected.

Asiya climbed aboard the bus with her oversized backpack bouncing against her shoulders. She found a seat by the window and waved excitedly with both hands.

Her parents waved until the bus disappeared around the corner.

For a few quiet moments, neither of them spoke.

Then Abi smiled.

“We’ve packed her backpack.”

Mama squeezed his hand.

“And with Allah’s help, we’ll keep packing her heart.”

Together they raised their hands and made du’aa’, asking Allah to protect their daughter, increase her in beneficial knowledge, surround her with righteous influences, and make them parents who continued to guide and support her every step of the way.

Kindergarten was only the beginning.

So was their partnership with her school.

And with trust in Allah, they were ready to take the next step together… Kindergarten Open House.

Author’s Note: Every school district is different. If your child has dietary, religious, or medical needs, don’t hesitate to contact your school before the first day. Building a positive relationship with your child’s teacher and school staff is one of the best ways to support your child’s success.

Sound Vision / Chicago

Our story,
since 1988.

Before there was a network, there was a home in Chicago. And a belief that Muslim families deserved media of their own.

Objects from the Sound Vision archive

A world of their own.

The story in chapters

  1. 1988

    Chapter 01

    It began at home.

    Abdul Malik Mujahid founded Sound Vision in Chicago, starting the work from his home. The ambition was to make media that would inform, educate and entertain Muslim families.

    Founding story · Sound Vision / Dallas Morning News, 2000

    Sources & archive notes
    • Sound Vision founding history ↗
    • Dallas Morning News, 2000 ↗

  2. 1989

    Chapter 02

    Faith meets the computer.

    QuranBase, Sound Vision’s first computer program, made the Quran searchable in English. HadithBase followed in the early 1990s.

    Ahmed Murad interview, 2000

    Sources & archive notes
    • Ahmed Murad interview, 2000 ↗

  3. 1990–91

    Chapter 03

    A puppet opens a door.

    Let’s Pray, Sound Vision’s first video, introduced Adam in 1990. Adam’s World launched as a series in 1991. Aneesah joined later.

    Sources & archive notes
    • Sound Vision introduction, 2000 ↗

  4. 1992–95

    Chapter 04

    Care reaches beyond the screen.

    Mujahid coordinated Bosnia Task Force USA. Working with the National Organization for Women, the coalition campaigned to have rape recognized as a war crime.

    Justice For All · Bosnia Task Force history

    Sources & archive notes
    • Bosnia Task Force history ↗
    • Founder biography ↗

  5. 1996

    Chapter 05

    A world comes online.

    The earliest archived soundvision.com homepage dates to December 1996. A world map, a child in motion and a colorful menu expressed a global, family-centered ambition.

    Sources & archive notes
    • December 1996 homepage ↗

  6. 1999

    Chapter 06

    A bright new doorway.

    Blue lettering and a vivid orange navigation bar welcomed readers to “Islamic Information & Products.” RadioIslam.com was offering internet radio by 1999.

    Sources & archive notes
    • 1999 homepage ↗
    • Radio Islam, 2000 ↗

  7. 2000s

    Chapter 07

    A shelf full of possibilities.

    Videos, software and recordings brought faith into everyday family life. The serif name and handwritten motto appeared on packaging before the 2015 website refresh.

    Sources & archive notes
    • Sound Vision FAQ, 2000 ↗

  8. 2004

    Chapter 08

    On air. And out in the world.

    Radio Islam’s first WCEV 1450 AM show aired in Chicago on October 1. MuslimFest launched in the Toronto area with DawaNet and Young Muslims Canada.

    Sources & archive notes
    • Radio Islam announcement ↗
    • MuslimFest anniversary report ↗

  9. 2015

    Chapter 09

    Building bridges of understanding.

    A new website brought the condensed serif name and multicolor script motto to the web. Blue and orange-red continued through the site’s design.

    Sources & archive notes
    • May 2015 homepage ↗

  10. 2016

    Chapter 10

    A voice in the public square.

    Sound Vision’s #ActualMuslims billboard campaign began in Chicago in August, with a blunt public rejection of ISIS. Billboards followed in other cities.

    Sources & archive notes
    • Medill, 2016 ↗
    • NBC Chicago ↗

  11. 2020

    Chapter 11

    A new screen. The same purpose.

    The Adam’s World app launched on April 6. Muslim Network TV soft-launched that April, carrying the media work into a new chapter.

    Sources & archive notes
    • Adam’s World app history ↗
    • Muslim Network TV launch report ↗

  12. TODAY

    Chapter 12

    Still helping tomorrow’s Muslims.

    Muslim Home, Adam’s World, Muslim Network TV, community programs and an extensive article library continue the work. Justice For All is an independent organization with shared roots.
